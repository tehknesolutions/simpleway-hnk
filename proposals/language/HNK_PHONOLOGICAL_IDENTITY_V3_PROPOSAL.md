# HNK Phonological Identity V3 — Universal Euphony Proposal

**Proposal ID:** `SWHNK-HNK-PHONOLOGICAL-IDENTITY-V3-PROPOSAL`  
**State:** `PROPOSED_AWAITING_HUMAN_GATE`  
**Supersedes:** nothing until approval  
**Current governed profile remains:** `SWHNK-HNK-PHONOTACTIC-PROFILE-V2`  
**Scope:** sound identity, authoring preferences, euphony scoring and external-resemblance governance only. It does not create semantics, grammar, morphology, curriculum authority, historical etymology or spiritual proof claims.

## 1. Design thesis

HNK is intended to be a universal constructed language that is pleasant to pronounce and hear, easy to sustain in connected speech, rhythmically memorable and capable of both everyday and sacred/poetic registers.

The target is not to imitate one source language. The target is an original HNK phonological identity informed by broad human-language reference families while preserving lexical independence.

**Core maxim:**

> Inspired phonologically by many languages; lexically independent of all.

## 2. Spiritual/aesthetic reference boundary

The creator identifies the Christian gift of tongues / spontaneous prayer speech as an important spiritual and experiential reference for HNK fluency.

For governance purposes this is treated as an **authorial spiritual-aesthetic design reference**, not as empirical evidence that a specific HNK form, phoneme, etymology or grammar has supernatural origin.

The operational qualities extracted from this reference are:

- spontaneous articulatory flow;
- breath-friendly syllabic sequencing;
- rhythmic repetition without monotony;
- transitions that can move naturally between speech, prayer, chant and song;
- alternation between soft/open and deep/resonant sound zones;
- low articulatory friction in ordinary vocabulary.

## 3. Reference-language influence model

Reference languages may contribute **design principles** but may not select HNK forms, meanings or etymologies.

### Flow / everyday accessibility references

- Esperanto: regularity, learnability and transparent structure as a comparison system;
- Japanese: smooth mora/syllable flow as a comparison target;
- Brazilian Portuguese: connected-speech musicality and vowel-rich accessibility;
- Tupi and other documented Indigenous Brazilian languages: comparative oral/phonological diversity, never guessed or generalized without source evidence;
- creole languages: comparative economy and analytic regularity, not direct copying.

### Biblical / classical conceptual references

- Biblical Hebrew: comparative Semitic phonology and biblical lexical tradition;
- Koine Greek: comparative classical/biblical phonology and conceptual vocabulary;
- Latin: comparative cadence and classical register.

### Deep / resonant phonetic references

- Arabic: comparative guttural, pharyngeal and uvular possibilities;
- Sanskrit and specifically identified South Asian languages: comparative systematic phonetic organization;
- Russian: comparative consonantal texture;
- Mandarin Chinese and specifically identified Sinitic varieties: comparative compactness and syllabic contrast; HNK does not inherit lexical tone automatically.

No reference language receives privileged authority to define HNK lexical forms.

## 4. Three phonological registers

The registers are **style-density zones inside one language**, not separate languages or dialects and not semantic determiners by themselves.

### LUMEN

Purpose: everyday speech, beginner vocabulary, children, rapid conversational use.

Preferences:
- high articulatory ease;
- open/resonant vowels;
- low cluster density;
- smooth CV-rich motion when available;
- limited density of marked/deep consonants;
- easy repetition in connected speech.

### LOGOS

Purpose: default neutral HNK, education, explanation, general vocabulary.

Preferences:
- balanced use of the HNK40 inventory;
- moderate phonological contrast;
- strong lexical distinctiveness without sacrificing fluency;
- neither artificially soft nor artificially marked.

### RUACH

Purpose: poetic, liturgical, theological, initiatory or high-symbolic vocabulary where separately governed.

Preferences:
- greater tolerance for deep/resonant phonemes already present in HNK40;
- greater articulatory contrast;
- slower or chant-compatible delivery may be appropriate;
- marked phonemes do not automatically imply sacred meaning.

A word's register must be assigned by explicit governance; phonetic shape alone does not create semantic or spiritual status.

## 5. HNK40 use policy

V3 retains all V2 segmental authority:

- G-ID + IPA remain authoritative;
- `G40 /y/` remains a vowel and must not be reinterpreted as `G16 /j/`;
- `TS = G30 /ts/` remains atomic;
- hiatus, final consonants and non-CV shapes remain legal where governed;
- no universal stress rule is created.

V3 adds one design objective: future HNK authoring should gradually use a broader governed portion of HNK40 rather than overfitting the language to only the 20 currently safe human-facing romanization units.

This expansion requires separate romanization governance for unresolved G-IDs before ordinary human-facing publication.

## 6. Euphony Score V1

Every newly authored lexical candidate should receive a 0–100 review score before human selection.

### Weighted dimensions

- **Pronounceability / articulatory ease — 20**
- **Connected-speech flow — 15**
- **Rhythm / musicality — 15**
- **Auditory memorability — 10**
- **HNK40 identity compatibility — 10**
- **Internal lexical distance — 10**
- **External lexical distance — 10**
- **Register fit — 10**

**Total: 100**

The score is a decision aid, not an automatic selector. Human review remains required.

### Suggested interpretation

- `90–100`: exceptional euphony candidate;
- `80–89`: strong candidate;
- `70–79`: acceptable with review;
- `60–69`: weak / revise preferred;
- `<60`: reject or redesign unless there is a specific governed reason.

No score may create semantic authority, historical status or productivity.

## 7. Internal Lexical Distance

New candidates must be checked against recovered and authored HNK forms.

Review dimensions:

- exact collision;
- edit-distance proximity;
- onset/rime similarity;
- repeated family-like shapes that might falsely imply morphology;
- excessive concentration of near-identical forms inside the same semantic domain;
- auditory confusability in connected speech.

Exact collision with an unrelated governed HNK form is normally a rejection.

Near collisions require explicit human review.

## 8. External Resemblance Risk — ERR

HNK must avoid accidental lexical dependence on reference languages and common international vocabulary.

### ERR classes

- **GREEN** — no salient external resemblance;
- **YELLOW** — weak resemblance; ordinary review;
- **ORANGE** — strong resemblance to a common external lexical form; explicit review required;
- **RED** — near-identity likely to create confusion, false-friend behavior, accidental borrowing appearance or undesirable association; reject or redesign unless explicitly governed as a loan/bridge.

### Initial external comparison set

At minimum, when reliable resources are available:

- Portuguese;
- English;
- Spanish;
- Esperanto;
- French;
- Italian;
- German;
- Biblical Hebrew transliteration;
- Koine Greek transliteration;
- Arabic transliteration;
- Japanese romanization;
- Mandarin pinyin;
- Russian transliteration;
- Sanskrit / explicitly selected South Asian transliteration;
- documented Tupi reference vocabulary where source quality is adequate.

The comparison set may expand later.

## 9. Cognate / false-cognate governance

### Principle 1 — no accidental cognate claim

Phonetic resemblance to another language never proves common origin, borrowing or semantic relationship.

### Principle 2 — no meaning by resemblance

An HNK form's meaning must never be selected because it resembles a foreign word.

### Principle 3 — intentional loans are exceptional

Any intentional loan, bridge or proper-name adaptation must be explicitly labeled and separately governed.

### Principle 4 — false friends are actively penalized

If a candidate is highly similar to a common foreign word with an unrelated or misleading meaning, ERR should rise to ORANGE or RED depending on salience.

### Principle 5 — universal does not mean average

HNK may feel internationally pronounceable while remaining recognizably HNK and not reducible to Esperanto, English, Japanese, Hebrew, Arabic or any other source language.

PT, EN and Esperanto remain semantic/reference layers only and do not generate the HNK form automatically.

## 11. Existing lexicon audit

Approval of V3 should **not invalidate** recovered or authored vocabulary.

Instead:

1. existing recovered forms remain in their current authority states;
2. existing AUTH candidates remain registered as-is;
3. selected-but-unregistered forms remain selected but unregistered;
4. a retrospective `HNK EUPHONY + ERR AUDIT` may classify existing forms;
5. any future replacement or revision of an existing form requires a separate lexical-authority gate.

No bulk renaming is permitted from V3 approval alone.

## 12. Explicit non-rules

V3 does not establish:

- sound symbolism as semantic evidence;
- sacred phoneme = sacred meaning;
- etymology by resemblance;
- direct borrowing from reference languages;
- mandatory CVCVCV structure;
- mandatory avoidance of marked phonemes;
- universal stress;
- universal morphology;
- universal syntax;
- supernatural linguistic proof claims;
- automatic replacement of existing words.

## 13. Proposed governance effect

If approved, V3 becomes the current **authoring identity layer** above Phonotactic Profile V2. V2 remains the factual/governed phonotactic substrate unless a later gate explicitly merges or supersedes its rules.

The immediate operational consequence would be:

- pause new HNK lexical candidate generation after already-open gates until Euphony + ERR review tooling/contracts are attached;
- preserve the Wave 04 selected-registration gate unchanged;
- prepare the 3000-slot PT–EN–Esperanto semantic master before large-scale Wave 05+ lexical generation;
- audit existing HNK forms without silently replacing them.
